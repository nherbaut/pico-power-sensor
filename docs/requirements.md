## Purpose and confirmed context

Build one bench instrument to compare the energy consumed by a Raspberry Pi 5 during workloads. Energy over time is the primary measurement goal. The instrument consists of one Pi power-and-sensing interposer and one separately powered microcontroller carrier.

This baseline records the owner's conversation answers and the starting requirements in [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md). Confirmed requirements, provisional targets and unresolved choices are distinguished below. Confirmation of a requirement does not establish that it is feasible or validated.

## Assembly and measurement constraints

- Assembly must be possible with a normal temperature-controlled soldering iron, without hot air, a microscope or reflow equipment.
- Use through-hole footprints on both custom PCBs, including headers for pre-assembled modules. Prefer replaceable sockets for ICs and modules. A fine-pitch device must arrive assembled on a suitable module or adapter; the owner must not have to solder that device onto an adapter.
- Follow AGENTS.md for permitted component families, soldering-tip clearance, pad and drill requirements, polarity markings and pin-1 identification. If a required function has no suitable part or module, report the gap rather than silently substituting an unsuitable package.
- Hand solderability is mandatory. The 100 mV drop and 5 mΩ reference are provisional targets; do not sacrifice assembly requirements to retain those values. Establish a final supply-voltage acceptance criterion before release.
- Keep instrument and DUT consumption distinct. The controller, monitor, storage, LEDs, protection bias, active interface supply and other auxiliary loads stay outside reported DUT current. The sole owner-approved UART-bias exception (2026-10-16) permits one 10 kΩ Pi-RX pull-up to Pi 3.3 V and two 100 kΩ pull-downs loading Pi TX/marker outputs. Include this small communication load in the DUT reading and its stated boundary; buffer ICs remain instrument-powered. No other Pi-powered interface-overhead allowance is adopted.
- Put protection and auxiliary supply draw upstream of the shunt, except for that named UART-bias network. Preserve four-wire Kelvin sensing and plan for a short, coupled sense pair separated from switching and high-current copper.
- Preserve the existing direct bench-supply arrangement. Do not substitute another supply or a voltage-converter HAT.
- Count the complete supply-path loss, including protection, shunt, positive and return copper, cables and connectors. Identify the voltage-sense location and which DUT-powered accessories are included before claiming DUT energy accuracy.

## Unresolved choices and acceptance criteria

The owner approved the [UART interface review](uart-interface-options.md)'s measurement-boundary exception with "ok" on 2026-10-16. The calculated nominal resistor load is 1.3068 mW on the 3.3 V side in the stated logic condition, before leakage, switching and Pi supply-conversion loss. This is the allowed network, not a requirement to fit every resistor. The updated single-buffer candidate in that review omits the Pi pull-up and calculates 0.2178 mW for the two nominal input pull-downs; the owner subsequently selected this circuit and SN74LV125AN with "ok". Neither figure is measured overhead, a full worst-case bound or a fixed correction to subtract. The remaining resistor/socket/capacitor parts, GPIO assignment, timing, power-state behavior and electrical qualification remain open; the bias exception did not itself select a device, but the subsequent single-buffer approval adds one SN74LV125AN to the BOM. See [UART qualification](uart-interface-qualification.md).
