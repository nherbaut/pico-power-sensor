# Protection screening: current-limit boundary

Date: 2026-10-16. This initial protection screen informed the subsequent owner-approved prototype choices: Littelfuse 021706.3MXP fuse, Schurter 0031.8201.G holder and Pololu 5383 assembled module. Selection does not resolve the qualification gaps below. In response to the proposed 5 A setting, the owner confirmed that power comes from the bench supply and its output current can be limited. **Use a 5 A current-limit setting as the operating condition.** This is owner confirmation of a setting, not a measured fault bound; tolerance, transient energy and output-shutdown behavior remain unverified. The DUT's peak duration and continuous-load envelope are separate open items.

Keep the required cartridge/through-hole fuse and hand-solderable reverse-polarity protection upstream of the shunt. Do not import the earlier project's bare fine-pitch controller/MOSFETs, voltage criterion, ambient range or electrical release evidence.

The subsequent [costed protection comparison](protection-options.md) records the owner's choice of Pololu 5383 with the cartridge fuse and OGN holder, including the approved holder hole exception, both compared module pinouts, preliminary planar fit, and unresolved sustained-fault qualification. The three chosen parts are in the provisional BOM.

## Findings to carry into selection

- A cartridge fuse rated above the supply's current limit must not be described as a guaranteed short-circuit interrupter. The earlier project's candidate **Littelfuse 021706.3MXP** led to checking the manufacturer's cached 217-series datasheet, revision GD 2025-07-17. Its 6.3 A row has 9.5 mΩ nominal cold resistance and a 70 A at 60 VDC interrupting-rating entry. The time/current table requires a 6.3 A fuse to carry 150% (9.45 A) for at least 60 minutes. This does not establish clearing at a supply-limited 5 A. The rating is an interruption capability under stated conditions, not a trip threshold. This fuse is a candidate for fresh qualification, not an inherited selection.
- At the 5 A setting, that 9.5 mΩ nominal cold value gives a **calculated 47.5 mV drop and 0.2375 W**, before warming, holder contacts, tolerances and other path losses. Combined with the nominal 5 mΩ shunt, the cold reference is **72.5 mV**, leaving only 27.5 mV of the provisional 100 mV total-path preference. This is not a worst-case budget or measurement. The fuse table separately specifies 130 mV maximum at its rated 6.3 A; do not substitute the nominal cold calculation for that operating specification or assume it establishes a hot 5 A bound. Do not raise the fuse rating simply to improve loss.
- The **Schurter OGN** family includes through-hole 22.5 mm variants 0031.8201 and 0031.8201.G; its current family datasheet distinguishes packaging variants. The ≤10 mΩ contact-resistance figure is specified at 100 mA. Its scope and behavior at 5 A need review; no 5 A drop bound or compatible lead-hole diameter is asserted here. Do not assume an old generic footprint or an SMD/THR family variant is suitable for hand assembly.
- A high-side P-channel TO-220 MOSFET is a plausible reverse-polarity direction. **Vishay SUP90P06-09L-E3** has a stated 11.8 mΩ maximum test-point resistance at VGS=−4.5 V, ID=−20 A, TJ=25 °C, using the datasheet pulse-test condition. Substituting that reference resistance into a 5 A screen gives **59 mV and 0.295 W**, not a guaranteed continuous 5 A/hot result. Its published hot limits are at −10 V gate drive and cannot establish our approximately −5 V case. Exact lifecycle, stock, lead drawing and startup/fault operation remain open; it is not selected.
- A simple reverse-input P-channel circuit is not automatically a reverse-current blocker, overvoltage protector or short-circuit disconnect. Under a hard output short, the gate/source voltage can collapse and the body diode can carry the supply-limited current. Check that sustained state and startup SOA, not only the normal channel I²R loss. Do not claim that a fuse above the current limit will clear it or that a short gate-charge delay is a guaranteed inrush limiter.

Sources are retained in [the protection manifest](../hardware/evidence/protection/sources.csv). The fuse PDF is a manufacturer work copied from the sibling cache, not newly fetched; the other two are manufacturer downloads. Tables were read for these limited claims. No package drawing, quantitative plot, complete protection circuit, thermal validation, native ERC/DRC or bench test has passed as a result of this screen.

## Fault cases required before recommending a circuit

| Condition | Design obligation | Present evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Correct input, normal load up to the proposed 5 A target | Include fuse, holder, semiconductor, shunt, wiring and both supply/return contacts in loss and thermal calculations | Nominal cold fuse/shunt calculation only; no complete budget |
| Correct input, hard output short | Tolerate sustained supply current limiting or implement a qualified disconnect; do not assume the 6.3 A fuse opens | Simple P-channel topology can lose gate drive and conduct through its body diode; sustained thermal case unresolved |
| Reversed bench leads | Keep the DUT and instrument interfaces within their permitted voltages in every power state | Current limiting alone does not establish polarity protection; circuit unresolved |
| Supply connection/startup or short applied | Account for output-capacitor discharge and control-loop response in semiconductor stress and fuse coordination | No supply transient measurements or applicable manufacturer bounds retained |
| Instrument USB on with bench supply off, or the reverse | Avoid back-powering and unintended high-current returns through sensing/UART/USB connections | Architecture requirement only; interface circuit not yet selected |

The supply-limit setting and prototype part choices are recorded. Qualification of the chosen fuse/holder and reverse-polarity circuit must still account for limit tolerance, transient/capacitor discharge behavior and any output-shutdown mode. Resolve the documented lifecycle, thermal, connection and footprint gaps before release. Retain all instrument bias consumption upstream of the shunt. A failed qualification must remain visible rather than being replaced by an assumed fast fuse opening or assumed supply shutdown.
